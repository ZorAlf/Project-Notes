Chapter 36 — Don't Become Slave to Pleasure

Dostoevsky - Don’t Become A Slave to Pleasure In The Brothers Karamazov, Dostoevsky compares two types of people: Worldly People and Monks. Worldly People chase pleasure and comfort, but in doing so, they lose their freedom. On the other hand, Monks renounce pleasure and, as a result, remain truly free. But why is that the case? According to Dostoevsky, freedom is not just the ability to do whatever you want, but the ability to endure suffering. If you can’t endure suffering, you become a slave to anything that protects you from it. Worldly People believe freedom lies in pleasure, so they dedicate their lives to acquiring wealth, status, luxury, and comfort.
